More recruiters rarely fixes a hard search

When a role stays open, the natural response is often to widen the vendor list. Add another agency, send the brief to three more recruiters, and hope somebody reaches a different pocket of the market.

That can create activity without creating coverage. If every recruiter approaches the same search as a fresh sourcing project, the hiring company gets more outreach, more duplicated candidates, and more people learning the role at the same time.

The useful question is not who can recruit this role. It is who is already close to these candidates.

A specialist recruiter starts from a different place. They understand the language of the role, know what adjacent backgrounds transfer, and often have relationships with people who are not waiting on a job board. That does not guarantee a placement, but it makes the first conversation materially more informed.

For the recruiter, specificity matters too. A clear search is more valuable than a vague promise of hiring. Location, level, compensation, technical must-haves, and urgency determine whether their network can actually help.

So I would rather make one introduction with a strong reason behind it than circulate the same requirement broadly. The leverage is in matching the hard part of the search to the recruiter who already lives there.

— I match specific hiring problems to recruiters already working that candidate market.
